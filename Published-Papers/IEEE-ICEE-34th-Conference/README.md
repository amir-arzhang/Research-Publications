## Abstract

This paper presents the design of a Variable-Order Fractional PID (VOFPID) controller for an Artificial Pancreas (AP) system. In contrast to existing approaches, the proposed structure adjusts the fractional integral and derivative orders in a time-varying manner based on the error signal and reference input signal to enhance controller flexibility and performance. The optimal controller parameters are tuned by the Genetic Algorithm (GA) by minimizing the Integral of Time-weighted Absolute Error (ITAE) criterion. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed controller not only reduces oscillations and achieves desirable transient behavior but also requires significantly less control effort. Based on these findings, the application of variable-order fractional calculus presents a promising avenue for the development of next-generation blood glucose regulation systems.

## Research Contributions

1. **Variable-Order Fractional PID Structure:** Development of a VO-FOPID controller with time-varying fractional integral and derivative orders adjusted according to the tracking error and reference input signal.

2. **Genetic Algorithm-Based Optimization:** Optimization of controller parameters using a Genetic Algorithm (GA) by minimizing the Integral of Time-weighted Absolute Error (ITAE) criterion.

3. **Improved Control Performance:** Simulation-based evaluation demonstrating reduced oscillations, desirable transient response, and significantly lower control effort.

## Methodology

The proposed control strategy consists of the following main components:

- Variable-Order Fractional PID (VOFPID) controller design.
- Time-varying adjustment of fractional integral and derivative orders.
- Genetic Algorithm-based parameter optimization.
- ITAE-based objective function for optimization.
- Numerical simulation and performance evaluation of the artificial pancreas system.

## Optimization Strategy

The Genetic Algorithm (GA) is employed to determine optimal controller parameters.

The optimization process is based on minimizing the Integral of Time-weighted Absolute Error (ITAE) performance criterion.

## Simulation Results

The simulation study evaluates the performance of the proposed VOFPID controller in artificial pancreas systems.

The main reported outcomes include:

- Reduced oscillations in blood glucose regulation.
- Desirable transient response.
- Improved flexibility through time-varying fractional orders.
- Significantly reduced control effort.
